02 / STORY

More than ten years after the events of the first film, Jake Sully lives with Neytiri and their children in the Omatikaya forest. But the “Sky People” have returned to Pandora — with new technology, new recombinant bodies and an old score to settle with Toruk Makto.

To spare the clan from disaster, the family travels east — to the reefs of the Metkayina, the people of the water. There the Sully children learn to hold their breath, ride the ilu and listen to the song of the tulkun. There, too, they learn the price of betrayal: the tulkun hunters are already out on the open sea.

“Family is our fortress. Our strength. Our weakness. Everything we have.” Jake Sully

Cameron shifts the register: instead of a war for the trees, it is a coming-of-age story of Lo’ak and Kiri, a clash between father and son, Neytiri’s maternal fury and the quiet grief of the tulkun Payakan. The finale unfolds inside a sinking ship — a ten-minute sequence for which the entire water simulation system was rebuilt.

Omatikaya

Omatikaya — “the Blue Flute clan”

A forest people who lived in Kelutral — Hometree — until it was burned. Hunters and archers bonded with the ikran (banshees) of the Hallelujah Mountains. After the exile the clan settled near the Tree of Souls, and it was here that Jake Sully became Toruk Makto — the Last Shadow Rider.

Metkayina — “the Reef clan”

A people of the eastern sea: broad forearms, tail-like braids, skin the color of sea water. They live on islands and in lagoons, dive dozens of meters deep and are bonded with the tulkun — creatures they regard as spiritual kin. Their tsahik Ronal keeps the memory of the clan.

Production dossier

How the water was filmed

Three years of preparation, four years of shooting and a technology that simply did not exist before “The Way of Water.”

01

Underwater performance capture

The actors did not perform in front of a dry “blue screen” but in a real tank: cameras, markers and lighting — all underwater. Cameron forbade “dry-for-wet” shooting, where actors imitate swimming on wires.

Motion capture
02

A 3.4-million-liter tank

900,000 gallons of water, wave generators, a controlled current and temperature. A separate tank was used for scenes inside the sinking ship, where the set was actually turned over.

900 000 gal
03

Variable 48 fps

HFR switches on in dynamic scenes and drops to 24 in static ones — this removes the strobing effect in underwater action shots without turning the film into a “broadcast.”

48 fps HFR
04

A new water simulation

Weta FX wrote the solver from scratch: spray, foam, caustics, refraction, interaction between bodies and the surface. A single shot of a tulkun alongside the ship took days to render on the farm.

Weta FX
05

Diver training

The cast went through freediving training: mask work, static and dynamic apnea underwater. Kate Winslet held her breath for 7 minutes 14 seconds — a record among the film’s actors.

Freediving
06

Glasses-free 3D on set

Cameron used a next-generation stereo system and monitored the depth of the shot on set, so that the depth of the scene was created during filming rather than in post.

Native 3D
